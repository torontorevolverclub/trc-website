Why These Rules Exist

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §1–2

Accident Prevention

The main purpose of these rules is accident prevention. Taught in person at the club, they also help new and prospective members learn the club’s rules, feel welcome and comfortable on the firing line, and ensures every shooter follows the same procedures.

Taking Responsibility

At TRC we keep the rules to the minimum consistent with good club management and safety. That only works when every member accepts responsibility for their own safety and that of others.

See something unsafe? Say something.

Most of us need a reminder once in a while. We’re proud of TRC’s safety record across more than 120 years — let’s keep it intact.

01 // Every Firearm, Every Time

ACTS

Source: Canadian Firearms Safety Course

The four basic firearm safety practices from the Canadian Firearms Safety Course. They apply to every firearm, everywhere, not just at the range.

A

Assume

Assume every firearm is loaded, even if someone tells you it isn’t.

C

Control the Muzzle

Control the muzzle direction at all times. Keep it pointed in a safe direction, generally down range.

T

Trigger Finger

Keep your trigger finger off the trigger and out of the trigger guard until you’re ready to fire.

S

See That It’s Unloaded

See that the firearm is unloaded: PROVE it safe. That’s the next section.

02 // The Core Skill

PROVE · Proving a Pistol Safe

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §4.3–4.5

“Proving” confirms a pistol is unloaded and safe. Remember it as PROVE. Do it every time a firearm changes hands — and don’t handle any firearm until you’re sure you know how to prove it.

  1. PSTEP 1

    Point in a safe direction

  2. RSTEP 2

    Remove any source of extra ammunition

  3. OSTEP 3

    Observe the chamber; remove any round, fired or unfired

  4. VSTEP 4

    Verify the feeding path is clear

  5. ESTEP 5

    Examine the bore; remove any obstruction

  1. 1

    Point the pistol in a safe direction.

  2. 2

    Locate and operate the magazine-release catch and the slide-stop catch.

  3. 3

    Remove the magazine — the source of additional ammunition.

  4. 4

    Lock the slide open, inspect the breech and remove any round, fired or unfired.

  5. 5

    Inspect the bore and remove any obstruction.

  6. 6

    Place it open, ejection port facing up, on a secure bench or table.

  1. 1

    Point the revolver in a safe direction.

  2. 2

    Open the cylinder and, using the ejector rod, remove all cartridges, fired or unfired.

  3. 3

    Inspect the breech and the bore; remove any obstruction.

  4. 4

    Place it, cylinder open, on a secure bench or table.

Never flip the cylinder in or out with a flick of the wrist. Always open and close it by hand. Flicking it can bend the crane, misalign the cylinder and spit lead shavings from the sides.
  1. 1

    Point the revolver in a safe direction and swing the loading gate aside.

  2. 2

    Inspect each chamber separately, using the ejector rod to remove every round, fired or unfired.

  3. 3

    Cycle through the cylinder twice to be sure every cartridge is out.

  4. 4

    Remove the cylinder from the frame and inspect the breech and bore for obstructions.

  5. 5

    It is only considered safe with the cylinder completely removed.

03 // Handling Pistols

Handling Pistols

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §4.1–4.6

Direction. Always handle a pistol so its muzzle points in a safe direction in case of an accidental discharge.

What “Safe” Looks Like

Revolver

Cylinder empty and open.

Self-Loader

Magazine out, slide locked back, ejection port facing up.

Single-Action Revolver

Cylinder removed from the frame.

Using Another Member’s Pistol

1

Ask First

The owner’s permission is required.

2

Owner Makes It Safe

On the line, benched, muzzle down range. Never handed over.

3

Owner Explains

Any peculiarities, like a set trigger or an unfamiliar safety.

4

You PROVE It

Step up to the line and prove it yourself before handling it.

Pistols are never passed hand to hand at TRC
30
Seconds
Hangfires

Hold It Down Range

If a round doesn’t fire, keep the pistol pointed down range for at least 30 seconds before opening the cylinder or breech. Modern ammunition rarely hangfires, but black-powder shooters still see them. Play it safe — it costs nothing.

Loading a Revolver

Right- and left-handed shooters load the same way, so the revolver is never passed hand to hand during loading.

  1. Grasp the revolver by the butt with the right hand. Unlock the cylinder with the right thumb and swing it out with the left hand.
  2. Pass the third and fourth fingers of the left hand under and around the cylinder, fingertips resting on a flute. This positions the cylinder the same way every time, even in dim light.
  3. Insert 5 rounds with the right hand while the left continues to hold the cylinder.
  4. With the left hand, gently close the cylinder, aligning the empty chamber with the bore and hammer, until it latches.

Never flick the cylinder shut with a snap of the wrist, as seen on TV. Close it gently by hand; flicking can bend the crane and make the revolver unsafe.

Cocking a Revolver for Single Action

Caution: a hammer can slip from under the thumb, and a finger can stray onto the trigger of a cocked revolver.

  1. Hold the revolver in the shooting hand, trigger finger along the side of the trigger guard.
  2. Grasp it with the non-shooting hand, fingers curled under the trigger guard, thumb just in front of the hammer.
  3. Cock with the shooting-hand thumb and keep it on the hammer spur until you slide the non-shooting thumb between hammer and frame.
  4. Adjust your grip and place the finger on the trigger to check it. With your thumb between hammer and frame it cannot fire.
  5. Take the finger off the trigger, remove the non-shooting thumb, come to the ready position, then put the finger back on the trigger.

Why reposition? Cocking a modern revolver moves the trigger back for the short single-action pull. Watch the muzzle.

Finishing with a Self-Loader

When you’ve finished, or the RO calls “Remove your equipment from the line!”:

  1. Make sure there is no round in the breech, keeping the pistol pointed down range.
  2. Insert an empty magazine, release the slide and pull the trigger to un-cock.
  3. Case the pistol, pack your remaining gear and clear the firing line.

Some antique and modern self-loaders can’t be handled this way. Owners are responsible for learning how to handle theirs safely and for following all RO commands.

04 // In Charge of the Line

Range Officer & Commands

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §3.4, §4.2, §5.8 · Range Regulations #5, #11

Range Officer on the line at all times. Whenever two or more shooters are on the range, one must be designated Range Officer. The RO is in charge of the range and responsible for safety on the firing line until they hand over to another shooter. Follow every RO command.

Cease Fire!

Everyone stops immediately, unloads, and makes their pistol safe.

Anyone who sees danger must call it — even off the firing line. “STOP”, “DON’T FIRE” or short whistle blasts work too.

Make Your Pistols Safe!

Unload, make safe and place on the bench, muzzle down range. Hands off.

You May Handle Your Pistols

Only now may you pick up your firearm again.

Load!

In competition, wait for this command before inserting a loaded magazine (ISSF: before even charging one).

Remove Your Equipment from the Line!

Make sure the chamber is empty, un-cock, case your firearm, pack your gear and clear the line.

05 // Shooting

On the Firing Line

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §5.1–5.7 · Range Regulations #6, #7

Muzzle Direction

Point down range at a right angle to your body, or down range toward the ground at about 45°.

5

Loading

Load only on the firing line: 5 rounds unless directed. Step out of your stance and face the target broadside so the muzzle stays down range. Count your shots.

Use the Shooting Bench

Stand right up to it: your arm hits the bench before the muzzle can drop toward your feet. Only your shooting arm may cross the firing line.

06 // On the Range

Targets & Equipment

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §3.2, §3.5 · Range Regulations #1, #9, #10, #22

Approved Targets Only

Only standard club-approved paper targets may be used. Shooting at cans, bottles or boxes creates ricochet hazards and is not permitted; anyone doing so may be banned from the range.

Eyes & Ears

Ear protection and safety glasses must be worn on the range. In the event of a ricochet, safety glasses could save an eye. Gun boxes that sit on the bench in front of you are encouraged over briefcases or tool boxes.

Range Access by Members

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §3, §3.3, §3.6–3.7

Your Access

Every member receives an access card for the clubhouse front door and a key for the target cupboard. Targets in the lock-up are free to members: please don’t waste them.

Range Hours

Members may use the range during posted hours. Other clubs rent the range on several evenings and have exclusive use. Closures appear on the club calendar.

Guests are welcome provided that they are directly supervised by members at all times.

Members may bring guests, or act as centrefire supervisor to other members, no earlier than six (6) months after acquiring full membership status including centrefire certification.

2

Guest Waiver Signed

Before the guest handles any firearm.

3

Supervised at All Times

Directly supervised by the host inside the club and on the firing line.

Never, Ever

Source: Safety & Familiarization Course §3–5 and club practice

  • Flip a revolver cylinder in or out with a flick of the wrist.
  • Bend down to pick anything up while holding a gun.
  • Reach past the firing line — not for live rounds, casings or anything else.
  • Bring ammunition to the Inspection Table.
  • Uncase a pistol anywhere except the firing line or the Inspection Table, including the sitting benches.
  • Pass a pistol hand to hand.
  • Leave a guest unsupervised, inside the club or on the line.
  • Pick up someone else’s firearm without permission.
  • Shoot at anything other than approved paper targets.
  • Bring any rifle other than .22 rimfire onto the range.
